Bringing gold into India — the rules

1

Duty-free jewellery

If you've lived abroad more than a year: 40 g (women) or 20 g (others) of jewellery, duty-free — no value limit since the Baggage Rules 2026 (from 2 Feb 2026).

2

Coins & bars

Gold coins, bars and biscuits get no duty-free allowance — they're always dutiable and must be declared.

3

Abroad 6+ months

"Eligible passengers" can bring up to 1 kg at a concessional rate — about 15% since 13 May 2026 — paid in foreign currency.

4

Short trips

On a holiday or business trip under 6 months, gold above the allowance is charged the baggage rate of about 38.5% — usually wiping out any saving.

Customs rules are simplified for guidance only and change often (the gold duty rose from 6% to 15% on 13 May 2026; some customs offices list 16.5% for passengers). Always check the latest CBIC rules and declare gold at the red channel — undeclared gold can be confiscated and fined. Gold rates: IBJA (India, excl. GST), Dubai City of Gold (suggested retail, excl. VAT), Gold Traders Association (Thailand), gold-api.com international price (US, UK, Singapore — dealers add a premium). Rates change through the day; check with the jeweller before buying.
